Across this product category, supplier datasheets typically publish a recommended dosage and a qualitative claim such as "heat resistant". The fields routinely withheld are active content, carrier identity, retention-versus-time data and shelf life. That is a defensible commercial choice — but it leaves a buyer comparing marketing language rather than data, and it pushes the comparison onto price per kilogram, which is the wrong unit for a low-inclusion additive.

Four questions worth asking any supplier

Including us. If a supplier cannot answer these, the comparison you are running is not the comparison you think you are running.

  • What percentage of the product is active, and what is the rest? Determines whether price per kilogram means anything.
  • What percentage is retained after conditioning at my temperature and residence time? Not "heat stable" — a number, tied to a defined process.
  • What is the stability at my warehouse temperature and humidity, over my time to consumption? A shelf life measured at 25°C does not describe a 32°C warehouse.
  • Will you supply a trial quantity small enough for me to verify this myself? The answer separates suppliers confident in their data from those relying on you not checking.

Our answer to the fourth is yes: trial quantities start at a single carton, with no minimum-order barrier to testing. The methods for running those checks are written up in the pelleting retention test and the accelerated storage protocol — both written so that you can run them without us in the room.

Frequently asked

Because a buyer cannot evaluate what a buyer cannot see. Active content, carrier identity and dosage split by application are the fields a formulator needs in order to decide, and withholding them means the comparison happens on price per kilogram instead — which is the wrong unit. We would rather be compared on the right basis and occasionally lose than be compared on the wrong basis and win.

It tells a competitor what our products do, which they could establish by buying a sample anyway. What it does not tell them is how the products are made. The information asymmetry that matters commercially is manufacturing know-how, not the datasheet.

Ask for four things in writing: the active percentage, the carrier identity, the retention percentage after your specific conditioning temperature and residence time, and the storage stability at your warehouse temperature and humidity. A supplier who can answer under NDA is being commercially careful, which is reasonable. A supplier who cannot answer at all has not measured it.
