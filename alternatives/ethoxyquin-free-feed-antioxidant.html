Ethoxyquin-Free
Feed Antioxidant

WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGES WHEN YOU SWITCH

Short answer: your cost per tonne goes up and your oxidation control gets harder to manage, because ethoxyquin was unusually effective per unit. What you buy in return is market access. Anyone telling you a replacement is both cheaper and equally effective is selling, not advising.

This page covers where ethoxyquin now stands market by market, what the authorised alternatives actually are, why dosage has to split by application, and how to verify oxidation control in your own operation rather than taking a datasheet on trust.

Key points

  • Ethoxyquin in the EU is permanently withdrawn, not suspended — Reg. (EU) 2017/962 suspended it, Reg. (EU) 2022/1375 made the refusal definitive.
  • Vietnam banned it across all feed from 1 April 2020, driven by shrimp export exposure.
  • The US still permits it up to 150 ppm; it remains permitted in China. Your constraint may be your customer's specification, not your own law.
  • EU-authorised synthetic feed antioxidants: BHA (E 320), BHT (E 321), propyl gallate (E 310). TBHQ is not among them, and does not appear in the US feed provisions of 21 CFR.
  • One dosage figure across all applications under-protects stored meals or over-treats complete feed. Ask for the split.

The pattern worth noticing: the EU decision propagates through supply chains rather than stopping at the EU border. Vietnam did not ban ethoxyquin because of a domestic safety finding; it banned it because Vietnamese shrimp goes to Europe. If you sell into any chain that terminates in the EU, the relevant document may be your buyer's specification rather than your own regulator's list.

2. What actually replaces it

OptionEU feed authorised?Practical note
BHA (E 320)Yes Combined limit with BHT applies; effective in fats and oils
BHT (E 321)Yes Widely used, usually blended rather than alone
Propyl gallate (E 310)Yes Sensitive to iron; needs a chelator alongside it in mineral-rich matrices
TBHQNo Listed for farmed animals in China's catalogue; not in EU feed authorisations; not in the US feed provisions of 21 CFR
Mixed tocopherols, rosemary extractYes, as feed materials or additives depending on form Different cost and stability profile; generally needs higher inclusion and careful storage
Chelators (citric acid, EDTA)Depends on the specific substance and form Not antioxidants themselves — they suppress metal-catalysed initiation, which is often the actual problem

The chelator row is the one most often skipped. Mineral premixes are full of iron and copper, and those metals catalyse the initiation step of lipid oxidation. An antioxidant system without a chelator is fighting a reaction that is being continuously restarted.

3. Why dosage must split by application

A single recommended figure across all applications is a simplification, and it fails in both directions: it over-treats complete feed and under-protects stored meals. Complete feed carries a few percent added fat; fishmeal and meat-and-bone meal can be highly unsaturated, and in a tropical warehouse they self-heat.

ApplicationWhy it differsSILSEP VSW5 published dosage
Complete feedLow added fat, short residence before consumption100–300 g/T
ConcentrateHigher fat and mineral load, longer chain to the mill600–1,000 g/T
PremixTrace metals concentrated; vitamins to protect600–1,800 g/T

Whoever you buy from, ask for the split rather than the headline. A supplier who can only give you one number has either not tested across applications or is not telling you which one the number came from.

4. Where SILSEP fits — and where it does not

VSW5 is a TBHQ + BHT + chelator system and contains no ethoxyquin. That makes it a straightforward answer for Vietnam, where ethoxyquin has been banned since 2020, and for the rest of Southeast Asia, South Asia and MENA.

It is not the right product for EU or US feed, because TBHQ is not authorised for feed in either. If that is your market, you need a BHA/BHT/propyl gallate system or a natural one — and we would rather tell you now than let you discover it at the port.

5. Verifying oxidation control yourself

  1. Run three arms. Untreated control, your incumbent, and the candidate — same batch, same day, same line.
  2. Accelerate. Hold sealed samples at 40°C and roughly 75% relative humidity.
  3. Measure a pair, not a single value. Peroxide value and anisidine value on the extracted fat fraction. Peroxide value alone can mislead late in the reaction, because primary peroxides decompose — a falling PV can look like improvement when it is the opposite.
  4. Sample at 0, 30, 60 and 90 days. Freeze each pull so all samples are analysed in one batch.
  5. Check the stored-meal case separately. If you buy fishmeal, the meal is where oxidation actually costs you — test it on its own, not only in finished feed.
