Comparing Palatants
Against Luctarom

HOW TO READ A RETENTION CLAIM PROPERLY

Read this first

Lucta is a serious formulator with a long track record in this category, and Luctarom is a credible product range. We have no head-to-head trial data against it and will not imply otherwise. What this page does is show you how to interpret the claims on both sides — ours included — and how to settle the question in your own mill.

1. The claim everyone quotes, and what it omits

The retention claim is the standard currency in this category: some percentage of original intensity retained after some number of days. Lucta publishes a claim of this shape for the Luctarom range. It is a reasonable thing to publish. But a retention figure is only interpretable with three attachments, and they are usually missing:

QuestionWhy it changes the answer
Held at what temperature and humidity? Reaction rate roughly doubles per 10°C. A figure from a 20°C dry store does not describe a 32°C, 85% RH warehouse — and no correction factor makes it transfer honestly
Measured how? Retained assay of a marker compound, sensory panel intensity, and actual feed intake are three different endpoints that can diverge. A product can keep most of its marker and still lose its feeding signal
Percent of what baseline? Pre-pelleting or post-pelleting changes the meaning completely. If the baseline is taken after conditioning, the pelleting loss has already been excluded from the number

This applies to our numbers too. When we say VS1437 holds 180 days under combined heat and humidity, you are entitled to ask the same three questions, and you should. The mechanisms behind all of this are written up here.

2. Carrier: the variable that decides humid-warehouse behaviour

Lucta describes a porous silica carrier at range level for Luctarom — more disclosure than most of this category offers, and worth acknowledging. The engineering trade-off is real and cuts both ways:

Carried on a porous substrateCarrier-free or matrix-protected
High internal surface area genuinely slows volatilisation — the molecule has further to travel to escape No inert mass to ship, clear customs on, or store
Easy to meter a gram-scale active at tonne scale Dispersion depends entirely on particle size distribution
Many carriers are hygroscopic — in 85% RH they pick up moisture, cake, and can raise water activity in the feed Different failure mode: fines and segregation if the granule is friable
Softer carriers abrade under mechanical stress; the active ends up somewhere other than the pellet Nothing to abrade off, but the active is more exposed if the matrix is breached

Neither form wins universally. What matters is whether the supplier has tested that specific form under your humidity and will show you the data.

5. Settling it in your own mill

  1. Match the sensory endpoint, not the dosage. Ask both suppliers for the inclusion rate that reaches an equivalent intensity in your formula.
  2. Split the batch and store it at your real conditions. 40°C and roughly 75% RH, sampled at 0, 30, 60 and 90 days, frozen at each pull.
  3. Blind sensory panel plus peroxide value. The first tracks the signal, the second tracks the damage. Full protocol here.
  4. Check caking and flow after storage, not only aroma. In a humid warehouse this is where a hygroscopic carrier shows up.
  5. Then run intake with a control. Analytical data tells you the product survived; only the animals tell you whether it works.

Trial quantities start at a single carton with TDS, SDS and COA, so the comparison does not require a commercial order. If Luctarom performs better in your mill, that is a legitimate result and worth knowing on a carton rather than a container.
