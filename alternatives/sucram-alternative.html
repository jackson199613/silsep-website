Looking for a Sucram
Alternative?

WHAT THE PUBLISHED DATA ACTUALLY LETS YOU COMPARE

Read this first

We are not going to tell you VS7188 is a drop-in replacement for Sucram. Nobody outside ADM can honestly claim that, because the current Sucram formulations are not published in enough detail to support an equivalence claim. What follows is what each side publishes, where the gaps are, and a protocol for getting a real answer in your own mill.

Sucram is ADM's feed sweetener range, originally developed by Pancosma. It is the reference point most buyers in this category compare against, and searches for an alternative usually come from one of three places: a regulatory change, a cost review, or a supply-chain decision to qualify a second source. Each of those needs a different answer.

1. The regulatory question, stated accurately

A lot of coverage says sodium saccharin has been "banned in EU feed". That is not what the instrument says. We read the original rather than the summaries:

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/1727 of 20 June 2024, withdrawing from the market certain feed additives. Annex Part 2, Artificial substances: E 954(iii) Sodium saccharin — Piglets. Adopted under Article 10(5) of Regulation (EC) 1831/2003, the procedural route for additives where no re-evaluation application was submitted or the application was withdrawn. Transitional measures (Article 2): existing additive stocks to 11 July 2025, premixtures to 11 October 2025, compound feed and feed materials to 11 July 2026. Read the original →

Three things follow. It applies to piglets, not all species. It is a procedural withdrawal, not a safety ban. And it applies equally to any saccharin-based sweetener — including SILSEP VS7188 and VS3965, both of which are built on sodium saccharin. If your problem is specifically EU piglet feed, we are not your answer either, and you should be looking at ADM's saccharin-free lines or an equivalent. We would rather say that than sell you something that cannot ship.

If your market is Southeast Asia, South Asia, the Middle East or North Africa, this regulation does not apply to you at all. Sodium saccharin remains listed for farmed animals in China's feed additive catalogue and is registered as a feed additive in the Philippines.

4. A switch trial that gives you a real answer

  1. Match the endpoint, not the dosage. Ask both suppliers for the inclusion rate that reaches an equivalent sweetness endpoint in your formula. Comparing at equal g/T compares nothing if the products differ in intensity.
  2. Run both through your own conditioner. Same day, same line. Assay the marker compound post-mixer and in the cooled pellet. The ratio is retention; the method is written up here.
  3. Check uniformity as well as retention. Ten sub-samples of finished pellet, assayed individually. The coefficient of variation is where carrier and particle size show up.
  4. Hold samples at your real storage conditions. Re-assess at 30, 60 and 90 days. Accelerated protocol here.
  5. Then run intake. With a control group. Sensory and analytical data tell you the product survived; only the animals tell you whether it works.

We supply trial quantities from a single carton, with TDS, SDS and COA, so that none of this requires a commercial order. If your incumbent wins, that is a legitimate outcome and we would rather you found out on a carton than on a container.
